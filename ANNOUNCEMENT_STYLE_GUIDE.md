# Announcement Style Guide

Voice: the app, talking directly to you.

## Title

- Short: 3-6 words, no trailing period.
- States the headline idea in its own words — doesn't just repeat the opening line of the body.
- Same playful voice as the body, not a flat category label ("New Feature", "Maintenance Notice").

## Body

- **Person**: First-person plural ("we"), addressing the reader as "you" directly. Never third-person or passive ("features were added").
- **Tone**: Warm, self-aware, lightly playful — never corporate or dry. It's allowed to have a personality and poke fun at itself.
- **Sentence shape**: One flowing sentence listing the announcement categories, joined with commas/"and" — mirrors "brag about X, give you a heads-up on Y, and keep you in the loop if Z."
- **Verbs over nouns**: "brag about," "heads-up," "keep you in the loop," "take an unscheduled nap" — active, conversational verbs instead of flat labels like "New Features" or "Maintenance Notice."
- **Personification for the unglamorous stuff**: downtime/bugs/maintenance get a human metaphor ("servers decide to take an unscheduled nap") rather than technical language ("scheduled downtime for server maintenance").
- **Bold for scannability**: Key nouns (the actual subject of each clause) are **bolded**, not whole phrases — lets someone skim and still get the gist.
- **Closing line**: A short, wry kicker in quotes that reframes the announcement as a small in-joke with the reader ("Consider yourself officially 'in the know.'"). Optional but a good closer for the first-of-a-kind or milestone posts.
- **Self-referential honesty**: Comfortable naming what the feature *actually is* in plain terms ("we finally gave this app a voice") rather than marketing-speak.

## Avoid

- Exclamation-point hype
- "We're excited to announce"
- Passive voice
- Jargon
- Apologizing excessively for downtime — acknowledge it lightly instead of groveling

## Example

**We Found Our Voice**

> We finally gave this app a voice. From now on, this is where we'll brag about **exciting new features**, give you a heads-up on **planned maintenance**, and keep you in the loop if the **servers** decide to take an unscheduled nap. Consider yourself officially "in the know."
